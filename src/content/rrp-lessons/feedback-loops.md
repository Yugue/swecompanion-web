## Feedback loops

## 1. Let yesterday's choice affect tomorrow's evidence

A **feedback loop** occurs when selection changes exposure and behavior, which then change training or future selection.

The loop can reinforce useful information or amplify a misleading signal.

## 2. Follow the cycle

```rrp-visual
feedback-cycle
```

The important feature is the return path: observed outcomes influence what becomes observable next.

## 3. Watch a count reinforce itself

At the same observed 5% CTR, A receives 1,000 displays and 50 clicks; B receives 100 displays and five clicks.

A learner treating total clicks as quality may give A still more exposure. Those counts do not prove A has greater intrinsic appeal.

## 4. Recognize a blind evaluation region

If training and evaluation use the same narrow policy, strong next-click results may say little about unseen inventory.

A model cannot receive logged credit for an unobserved alternative's unknown outcome. The offline chapter explains the resulting evaluation limits.

## 5. Monitor opportunities alongside outcomes

| Observation | Companion check |
|---|---|
| Few items dominate clicks | Their impression shares and audiences |
| New items collect little engagement | Whether they were shown |
| Lists narrow over time | User intent and satisfied engagement |

Concentration alone does not prove a harmful loop. Demand, eligibility, and item purpose can explain some patterns.

## 6. Create evidence about alternatives

Controlled exploration changes opportunities to observe uncertain eligible items. Suitable experiments can compare effects over time.

Fresh-item budgets or variety rules change exposure but are not automatically unbiased experiments. Preserve the selection process and probabilities when the evaluation requires them.

## 7. Interview reasoning

**Question:** Why is replacing total clicks with CTR not a complete feedback-loop fix?

**Answer:** CTR removes one volume effect, but audiences, placement, and uncertainty still differ. The system continues controlling which alternatives receive evidence.
